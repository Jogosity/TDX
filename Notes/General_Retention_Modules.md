
###### Objective and Strategy
We will be making a suite of games in different genres that will all utilise the same format of Retention Modules. These will be implemented in ways that fit the core mechanics and genre of each game, but the intent and form of the modules will be common.

# Generic Overviews of Retention Modules

##### **Module 1: Upgrade Path**

**Pattern:**
	Allow the player to invest earned resources in upgrades to overcome rising PVE difficulty. Power grows vertically - bigger numbers, better gear, higher stats. There is a smaller horizontal spread in the Upgrade Path, sometimes the availability of new weapons or powers lives here. But mostly, the Upgrade Path just means bigger and better.


**How it Retains**
	Creates a sense of investment and sunk cost. Players return to protect and extend what they have built. Extremely clear challenge/fail/upgrade/victory loop as a motivation.  The gap between current power and next challenge becomes daily motivation and intent.
	Exposed values can clarify difficulty reqs to offer distinct milestones. (Eg. Level 3 - Enemy Power: 100)

**Key mechanics**
 - Primary stat increase - health/damage etc
 - Skill Trees and Skill Points
 - Star/Ascension systems (upgrade same item multiple times to even higher mastery levels)
 - Bonus abilities/buffs/debuffs
 - Cheap content - make numbers bigger on existing game challenge to force player to engage in upgrade path

**Example Games**
 - Star Wars Galaxy of Heroes - Level-up a specific character
 - Archero 2 - Level up gear
 - Destiny 2 - Find/infuse higher powered weapons and armour. Increase core Light/Power level

**Best suited for:**
	Action games where multiple, incremental improvements is a meaningful progression metric. Low emotional impact, high tactical impact.



##### Module 2: Collection (Core)

**Pattern:**
	Collecting is a central or otherwise essential mechanic to the game. Expanding roster of cards, characters, vehicles or weapons where each new unlock allows more fun - or existing fun to be replayed in a remixed format. Higher emotional aspect to the decision process of what to collect. Self expression lives here.
	Power grows horizontally - expanding options and flexibility of game tactics


**How it Retains**
	Heavily choice-based. Personal decisions predominant. Most effective in games where the collection space is much wider for most players to clean-sweep. There is a constant range of content to chase. New unlocks grant meaningful difference to the game experience.

**Key mechanics**
 - New characters/vehicles/weapons - often with unique and new abilities
 - Gacha/loot drop - randomised acquisition - often tethered to replay a specific challenge to get the loot you want.
 - Rarity tiers - Basic/Common/Rare/Legendary/Exotic (grey/green/blue/purple/gold)
 - Expensive content - needs unique design per unlock

**Example Games**
 - Star Wars Galaxy of Heroes: unlocking new characters and entire teams
 - Pokémon Go: increasing your roster of Pokémon to fill the Pokédex
 - Marvel Snap: card collection drive deck-building strategy
 - Destiny 2: Chasing new weapons with unique traits. Acquiring Special elemental skills.
 - Forza Horizon: New cars are the game, find/buy/drive is the fun loop.

**Best suited for:**
	CCGs are the natural home, as are RPGs and strategy games where an expanding roster widens the game space in a meaningful way.


##### Module 3: Area Unlock

**Pattern:**
	Progression expands the playable space. New zones, larger maps, new biomes. The world literally grows as the player invests time. Often includes visual refresh.


**How it Retains**
	Sense of real progression. Area expansion can be related to narrative progress. This is often a very intrinsic motivation as it has a more tangible, often emotional core that exceeds the "more stuff/bigger numbers" oriented patterns. Area unlock often comes with new challenges that require investment in Upgrade Paths or Collection (Core) activities in a powerful recursive loop.


**Key mechanics**
 - Level progression - linear progress through visual, mechanical and narrative space
 - New challenges/enemies/difficulty
 - Pure exploration, often with a sense of freedom
 - New items to find and collect - and excuse/requirement to use the new stuff
 - Map expansion - farm/base-building games
 - Seasonal even areas (often temporary)

**Example Games**
 - Star Wars: Jedi Survivor - new planets, progressing narrative
 - Halo - classic linear level and narrative continuity
 - Stardew Valley - new areas to farm area expands, new areas to explore/exploit
 - Legend of Zelda - whole new areas to explore
 - Forza Horizon - new roads to drive, races to partake in


**Best suited for:**
	RPGs are the natural home. But linear action games also live or die by the effectiveness of their area expansion. Farming games also depend on this mechanic as do some tycoon games where a bigger factory can offer better machines etc.


##### Module 4: Re-engagement

**Pattern:**
	A combination of economic help and outright bribery. Gifts and rewards are given for a variety of game activities, sometimes even completely trivial activities.
	Daily log-in rewards and daily quests and unbroken streaks are the bread and butter of Re-engagement.
	Cheap mechanics to implement. Re-uses existing economic structures. Sense of FOMO and obligation are the primary motivational drivers.


**How it Retains**
	Free gifts feel good. Trivial gifts are often wrapped up in dopamine-friendly reward reveal feedback. Time-limited streaks have a "may as well" completion motivation, long streaks dip into a more intrinsic motivation about personal pride.
	Repeatable dailies can offer structure to players who want to just play, but not engage on deeper or higher end challenge.

**Key mechanics**
 - Daily Login - a single repeating item or currency, or a ramping item rewarding a 7 day unbroken streak
 - Extended streak - Duolingo style lifetime streak tracking. Heavily promoted in UI
 - Streak freezing and recovery
 - Daily Quests - A set of repeatable in-game quests, usually pulled from pretty generic activities. 
 - Battle Pass - bridges from Monetisation, but is mainly about Engagement. Not just FOMO but actual loss of value if a Battle Pass day is missed.

**Example Games**
 - Duolingo - not a game, but the powerful use of lifetime streak is best-in-class.
 - Star Wars: Galaxy of Heroes - Extensive daily quest list
 - Archero 2 - Multiple App and per-feature logins with numerous, variable length Battle Passes.
 - Forza Horizon - daily spin for new car
 - Destiny 2 - Season Pass, daily/weekly portal activities

**Best suited for:**
	Extremely flexible mechanic. Often used in Mid-core games where the players may have started as Casuals but have been graduated to Dedicated and Retained players. (Eg Fortnite, Archero2, SWGoH). Pan-genre. Seen on desktop, console and mobile surfaces. Once-were-casual audiences can quickly return to casual if the get disaffected so lots of effort is needed to keep them.


##### Module 5: Collection (Secondary)

**Pattern:**
	Collectible sets of items with no- or low-impact on actual gameplay.  Often based entirely in UI in the form of stickers or Grimoires of narrative detail.


**How it Retains**
	Usually little more than a tertiary mechanic, this is for more dedicated players who are digging into UI screens. This can add some Time-Spent if that is important to you. It also can have some extra reward pops at the end of a level and is very scalable. Can be linked to buffs and bonuses if you have enough secondary Collectibles. Well balanced, this system could provide a one-more-go, or a targeted one-more-go if the locations of various stickers or grimoires are semi-predictable.

**Key mechanics**
 - Random dropped items.
 - Hidden items
 - UI screens that expose the collections with X/Y counters
 - Secret sequence (fight here, wait 30 seconds, tap that...)

**Example Games**
 - Halo Infinite - Audio Logs
 - Disney Solitaire - collectible scene features
 - Stardew Valley - all items collection list

**Best Used in**
Casual games if they are just stickers/2D assets. Mid-core and hard-core if they are Grimoires with lore attached.
